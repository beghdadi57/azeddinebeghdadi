Azeddine Beghdadi is Professor of Visual Computing and Signal Processing at Université Sorbonne Paris Nord, where he has held a professorship since 2000. He was promoted to Exceptional Class Professor in 2016.

He is a founding member of the Laboratory of Information Processing and Transmission (L2TI) and served as its director between 2010 and 2016. He also founded the European Workshop on Visual Information Processing (EUVIP) and chairs its steering committee.

His academic background includes graduate studies in optics and signal processing, followed by a PhD in the same field. His research has progressively connected computational visual perception, bio-inspired modelling, physics-based image analysis and modern machine-learning approaches.

His scientific activity covers visual quality assessment, restoration and enhancement, scene understanding, medical imaging and robust computer vision.

Research Interests

Areas of Expertise

Research activities cover perceptual models, quality assessment, physics-guided image analysis and learning-based methods for robust visual understanding.

◐

Image & Video Quality Assessment

Development of full-reference and no-reference perceptual quality measures for natural images, medical imagery, surveillance video and document content, together with dedicated benchmark datasets.

◑

Bio-inspired Vision Models

Computational modelling inspired by retino-cortical mechanisms such as adaptation, contrast perception, inhibition and visual attention, including their integration into modern vision architectures.

◒

Physics-based Image Analysis

Analysis and restoration of visual information acquired in difficult conditions such as haze, rain, blur or low illumination using physical models and learned inverse methods.

◓

Medical & Laparoscopic Imaging

Image enhancement and perceptual quality analysis for laparoscopic and capsule endoscopy imagery, with applications developed in collaboration with medical and clinical partners.

◕

Robust Computer Vision

Object recognition and detection under realistic acquisition degradations and scene-dependent distortions, including benchmarking through datasets such as CD-COCO and VSQuAD.

◖

Self-Supervised Representation Learning

Learning representations through domain-informed objectives and consistency constraints for visual signals and time-series data.
